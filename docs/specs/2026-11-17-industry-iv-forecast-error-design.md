# Industrial IV with the forecast error, holding realised weather fixed (proposal)

**Status: proposed 2026-11-16, not registered. Binding when the user confirms and this file is committed; the run follows that commit.** Follows `2026-11-17-industry-iv-estimation-design.md` (V3 elasticity +0.005 [-0.005, +0.023], but the placebo outcome failed: instruments predict load 7 days earlier, -0.022, p 0.002; years unstable; solar-only weak). Realised wind and solar are `data/ree/ree_realtime_5min.parquet` (`wind`, `solar_pv`, MW, averaged over the local hour); forecasts are ESIOS 1777 and 1779.

**Why the earlier design failed where step zero passed.** Step zero asked 'is the design strong and precise?' (first-stage F, size of the standard error); it passed. It never asked 'are the instruments valid?'. The E-rules ask that (placebo outcome, overidentification, stability), and the placebo failed: the forecasts also describe the *weather* (a windy week is a week with a particular temperature and cloud pattern), and industrial load responds to weather, so part of the forecast's effect on load is not price. The fix has to separate the forecast from the weather it describes.

**In one example.** On Tuesday at 3 pm REE forecasts 6 GW of wind on D-1 and bidders price accordingly; the wind that actually blows is 8 GW. The 2 GW miss is not weather you could have known the day before: it moves the day-ahead price (set on the forecast) and, once the actual wind of 8 GW is held fixed, does nothing else to a steel plant. Two days with the same actual wind and different forecasts differ in price but not in weather: that is the variation we use.

**Important clarification of the instrument.** The forecast error by itself does **not** move the day-ahead price (the price is set before the error exists). It moves the price only *conditional on realised weather*: price depends on the forecast, F = realised + error, so with realised wind held fixed the error is the forecast's movement. The design therefore uses the error as instrument and puts realised wind and solar in the controls; it is not a claim that errors move prices unconditionally.

## Setting
Same sample as the estimation (non-holiday weekdays 2019-01-01 -> 2023-12-31, lockdown block out), same outcome (ln load of ESIOS 1204-1207), price (OMIE day-ahead), mean price €98 to convert to an elasticity, **short-run impact elasticity**. Instruments: error_w = wind forecast - realised wind, error_s = solar forecast - realised PV, and their squares (GW). Controls added to the V3 predictors of the boosted learner (the differenced pipeline, with differenced controls): realised wind and realised PV (levels and squares, at the hour and the day mean), on top of temperature, demand forecast, load and price lags 7/14/28 days, gas price. V1 (week-on-week difference with OLS controls including realised wind and PV) is the secondary variant; V2 is not repeated. Cross-fitting, folds, seeds and randomization inference (1,000 permutations of the residualised instruments across days within year-month, hour matched; p from the rank of |t|, interval from the permutation Anderson-Rubin set, grid ±40 standard errors) as in the estimation spec.

## Rules, set before the run
- **Q1 First stage (stop rule).** Date-clustered first-stage F of the price on the residualised error instruments is at least 10, and detectable elasticity (2.8 × SE) is at most 0.25. *If Q1 fails, no slope is computed:* the error is too small a source of price variation; the industrial IV is closed with the near-zero bound from the forecast design, stated with its exclusion caveat, and the first-stage F and detectable elasticity are recorded.
- **Q2 Balance (exclusion diagnostic).** The residualised error instruments are not predicted by weather: a date-clustered joint F test of the residualised instruments on temperature (hour, day mean, minimum, maximum, squares), p above 0.05. *Fails:* the error itself contains weather information (forecast misses happen in particular weather regimes), so the exclusion restriction is not repaired by controlling realised wind and PV; the temperature-related part is then added to the controls in a separate, registered step.
- **Q3 Placebo outcome.** Same pipeline with the outcome ln load of the same hour 7 days earlier: randomization p above 0.05 and absolute 'elasticity' below 0.01. *Fails:* the problem is not only the weather the forecast describes; then the instruments pick up a persistent regime beyond realised wind and PV.
- **Q4 Estimate.** V3 impact elasticity (error design) is negative, absolute value at most 1, and its randomization p is at most 0.05 with the interval excluding zero. *Fails with Q3 passing:* a clean near-zero industrial response, reported as a bound; *with Q3 failing:* not reported as an elasticity.
- **Q5 Overidentification.** Hansen J p above 0.05, wind-error-only and solar-error-only slopes inside each other's randomization intervals (solar-only is reported as weak if its F is below 10, and the rule is then judged on J alone).
- **Q6 Stability.** Year-by-year slopes (2019, 2021, 2022, 2023) have the same sign and equality p above 0.05. *Fails:* the response differs by regime; reported by year.
- **Q7 Comparison with the forecast design.** The error-design elasticity lies inside the randomization interval of the forecast design [-0.005, +0.023], and V1 agrees with V3. This is a description, not a pass/fail on identification: moving from the forecast to the error shows how much of the earlier near-zero was weather.
- **Q8 Hour blocks (exploratory, no pass/fail).** Night, day and evening blocks.

A failed rule is recorded and kept; no other instrument or learner is tried after a failure.

## What each outcome means
Q1-Q6 pass: a clean industrial impact elasticity, comparable with the household one. Q3 passes and Q4 shows a near-zero: the industrial hourly response is bounded near zero, which with the household -0.10 is the sector contrast. Q1 fails: closes the industrial IV with the bound only. Q2 or Q3 fail: points to the next repair named under the rule.

## Threats
Realised wind and PV are from REE's real-time system (peninsular, may differ slightly from the forecasting target); the forecast error is correlated with unobserved local weather; boosted controls can leave non-linear weather effects; the price still reflects the *forecast of the whole system* (demand, hydro), so the error identifies the price effect of wind and solar misses only; the realised-wind control uses the same hour and cannot remove day-level weather that is a function of the forecast regime; the first stage may be weak because errors are small (corr(forecast, realised) 0.965 wind, 0.992 PV).

## Result (run 2026-11-16; script `industry_iv_forecast_error.py`, committed before the run)

**Q1 fails: first-stage F 6.95 (bar 10); standard error of the elasticity 0.0188, detectable 0.053 (passes the 0.25 bar).** Stop rule applied: no slope was computed or stored, and Q2-Q8 were not run. Output `outputs/tables/industry_iv_forecast_error_checks.csv` holds the Q1 row only.

**Reading.** Once realised wind and PV are held fixed, the forecast error moves the day-ahead price too little for the instrument to be strong (the forecast and the realised output correlate at 0.965 and 0.992, so the error is a small part of the forecast's variation). The earlier design's strong first stage (F 94) came largely from the forecast carrying the weather itself, which is what also made it fail the placebo. So the strength and the invalidity had the same source: a clean instrument is weaker. This is the cost of repairing exclusion by conditioning on realised weather.

**Consequence under the spec.** The industrial IV by this route is closed. The statement that stands is the bounded one from the forecast design, with its exclusion caveat: V3 elasticity +0.005 [-0.005, +0.023], placebo failed, years unstable; no detectable hourly industrial price response larger than about 2% per 100% price change, not reported as an identified elasticity. **Open idea, not registered:** an instrument built from the day-ahead *bids* (re-clear the market with offered wind and PV volumes replaced by realised output; the price change is a merit-order-weighted supply shock) could be stronger than the linear error because it follows the slope of the curve; it needs its own spec and a reliable wind/PV unit list.
