# The Spanish time-of-use tariff reform: legal sources (read in the BOE, 2026-11-12)

What the reform is, with the primary text for each step of the charge path. Read from the BOE's own pages (`https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=<BOE id>`). Quoted amounts are €/kWh as printed; €/MWh = ×1000. The BOE's consolidated pages are informative, not legally binding.

## The two components, and where they come from

The ESIOS series 1876 ("Desglose peaje por defecto 2.0TD: Peajes y cargos") is the sum of two regulated parts, set by different bodies:
- **Tolls (peajes de acceso)**, the transport and distribution network charge: CNMC, methodology Circular 3/2020, values by resolution.
- **Charges (cargos del sistema)**, the system charge (renewable support, deficit, non-peninsular costs): Government, methodology Real Decreto 148/2021, values by ministerial order.

Tariff segments in the charges orders are numbered 1–6 and are **not named** in the text. Segment 1 (three energy periods, two power periods) is 2.0TD by shape; segments 2 and 3 (six periods) are 3.0TD and 6.1TD by shape. The mapping is an inference from period counts, **but it is confirmed numerically for segment 1**: toll plus charge reproduces ESIOS 1876 exactly (below).

## What this settles

1. **Start date.** All tariffs, including 3.0TD and 6.x, apply the new tolls from 1 June 2021 (one resolution, one date clause). The earlier open item is closed.
2. **The 16 September cut.** It is a decree-law provision (RDL 17/2021, DA3), from 16 September to 31 December 2021, to the **charges only**; the network tolls stay. That explains why 1876 falls 76% at peak while the charges fell 96%: the toll part (27.4 of 133.1 at peak) did not move.
3. **The paper's 96%.** It is the charges (RDL 23/2021 preamble), not total tolls and charges. The paper's "96% reduction of system charges" and our −76% are consistent.
4. **ESIOS 1876 is tolls plus charges**, to the cent, for summer 2021.

## Still open

- The **1 April 2022 step** in 1876 (peak 100.8 → 74.4) is not in the 2022 charges order (single price for the year). The source is not identified; likely a toll update or another decree-law. Not to be cited as a policy until found.
- The 2022 charges plus the summer-2021 tolls give 100.3 at peak against 100.8 in 1876, so the tolls probably changed slightly on 1 January 2022; the 2022–2026 toll resolutions were not read.
- The calendar of the six periods (Circular 3/2020, art. 7) is not reproduced in the resolution; needed for any 3.0TD/6.xTD design.
- Segment-to-tariff mapping for segments 2 and 3 is by period count only.

This lowers the expected effect and the power of an industry design.

**What this changes in the design.** (1) Industry did not have flat prices, but the reform still moved the price shape a lot for two groups: **3.0 (low voltage, above 15 kW) and 6.4 (145 kV and above)**; the middle tariffs (6.1 to 6.3) moved about a third as much in log terms. The dose is not monotone in voltage, which is useful: a response that follows the dose should be largest for 3.0 and 6.4 and smaller in between, not simply falling with voltage. (2) The 3.0 customers sit in **E0** (below 1 kV) with the households, so E0 mixes a 2.0TD group (dose: flat 44 → 133/42/6) with a 3.0TD group (dose: +1.34 in the log ratio). (3) Average energy prices rose for every tariff (the reform moved cost from power terms to energy terms), so a level effect comes with the shape effect; the power terms changed too and are not in this table. (4) **Still needed:** the hours of the old periods (the orders point to Orden ITC/1659/2009 chapter VI and RD 1164/2001, not read), without which the old price cannot be assigned to an hour; and the old toll for 6.1B (30–36 kV) and the E2/E3 boundary.

## Old period hours and the schedule module (read 2026-11-12)

**Source:** Orden ITC/2794/2007, de 27 de septiembre (BOE-A-2007-17078), annex II, read from the BOE's consolidated PDF (PyMuPDF). It defines the seasons, day types and hours of the access tariffs in force until 31 May 2021 (the consolidated text is assumed unchanged to that date; later amendments were not checked).
- **3.0A and 3.1A (three periods), peninsula:** by clock time, every day (**no weekend distinction**). Winter clock: P1 (peak) 18–22h, P2 8–18h and 22–24h, P3 0–8h. Summer clock: P1 11–15h, P2 8–11h and 15–24h, P3 0–8h.
- **6.1A, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4 (six periods), peninsula:** seasons December–February type A (P1 10–13h and 18–21h, P2 8–10h, 13–18h and 21–24h); second half of June and July type A1 (P1 11–19h, P2 8–11h and 19–24h); first half of June and September type B (P3 9–15h, P4 8–9h and 15–24h); November and March type B1 (P3 16–22h, P4 8–16h and 22–24h); April, May and October type C (P5 8–24h); **weekends, national holidays and all of August type D (P6 all day)**; P6 is 0–8h on every other day.
- Implications: in the old regime August was off-peak all month and the evening peak for 6.x was 18–21h in winter only, whereas the new regime has August in the medium season with 18–22h in P3/P4; the old 3.0/3.1 peak was 11–15h in summer, not the evening.

**`analysis/tou_tariff_schedule.py`** (peninsula) gives the old and new period and price by date and hour for each tariff and writes `outputs/tables/tou_tariff_hourly_change.csv` (month × day type × hour × tariff, 2019 calendar). Checks pass: every hour is assigned and the periods average 24 hours a day under both regimes. 3.1A customers become 6.1TD, so they are a separate row (`3.1->6.1`).

**What it shows (July working days; change in the log of the toll price, evening 18–22h against night 0–8h).** 3.0: +1.74; 3.1→6.1: +2.37; 6.1: +0.56; 6.2: +0.61; 6.3: +0.56; 6.4: +1.22. **But this overstates the dose for industry, because a toll difference of €10–70 sits on top of an energy price of about €100.** With an illustrative energy base of €100/MWh the change in the log relative *total* price (evening against night, July) is about: **2.0TD households +0.61** (as in the household design); **3.0: +0.40; 3.1→6.1: +0.34; 6.1: +0.22; 6.2: +0.12; 6.3: +0.09; 6.4: +0.06**. So in economic terms the dose **falls with voltage** (not the non-monotone pattern of the toll ratios): the series for E0 (2.0TD plus 3.0) and E1/E2 (3.1→6.1 and 6.1) have large doses, E3 and E4 small, E5 and E6 tiny. **This is the dose-response gradient to test, with the confound that flexibility also tends to fall with voltage** (large continuous processes), so a falling response with voltage is not proof of a price response. The calculation used a flat €100/MWh and one month; the real design needs the hourly wholesale price (ESIOS 1001's energy part, or the day-ahead price) and each contract type's exposure.

## Area weather (2026-11-12)
`scripts/build/build_area_weather.py` → `outputs/tables/regulated_area_panel/area_temperature_hourly.parquet` (area, hourly UTC, 2018-01 → 2026-05, no gaps). The paper uses population-weighted MERRA-2 per area; MERRA-2 needs a NASA Earthdata login (not enterable here), so the 21 ERA5 city series already on disk (`temperature_population_hourly.parquet`) are weighted by city population and an approximate city-to-area assignment (Endesa: Barcelona, Zaragoza, Seville, Málaga, Granada, Córdoba; Iberdrola: Madrid 70%, Valencia, Alicante, Bilbao, Valladolid, Murcia, Pamplona; Naturgy: A Coruña, Vigo, Madrid 30%; EDP: Gijón; Repsol/Viesgo: Santander; Portugal: Lisbon, Porto, Braga, Coimbra). Limits: a handful of cities per area, shares of Madrid set by hand, ERA5 not MERRA-2. Area correlations 0.83–0.99 (Spanish areas 0.86–0.99, Portugal 0.83–0.93): the series do differ across areas, most for EDP/Repsol (north coast) against Endesa/Iberdrola.

**MERRA-2 area weather (2026-11-12, the paper's source).** `scripts/fetch/fetch_merra2_t2m.py` fetches MERRA-2 `M2T1NXSLV` v5.12.4 hourly 2 m temperature (T2M) over Iberia (35.5-44 N, 10 W-4 E) from NASA's Earthdata Cloud OPeNDAP with the user token (`EARTHDATA_TOKEN` in `.env`; the old goldsmr4 host answers 410), 1,461 days 2018-01-01 → 2021-12-31, 86 MB in `data/merra2/raw/`. Files are named `MERRA2_400` except September 2020 and June-September 2021 (`MERRA2_401`); a first run missed those 152 days, the patched fetcher tries both. `scripts/build/build_area_weather_merra2.py` interpolates the 0.5 x 0.625 degree grid to the 21 cities, weights as the ERA5 version, labels each hourly mean by its hour start (MERRA-2 stamps HH:30), and writes `outputs/tables/regulated_area_panel/area_temperature_merra2_hourly.parquet`. Limit unchanged: city points weighted by population, assignment of cities to areas approximate (not the paper's population-weighted gridded areas).
