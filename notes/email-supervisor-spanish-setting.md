**Subject:** Progress — data gathered, coverage problem solved

Dear [Professor],

A short update on where things stand.

I've assembled the data for the Iberian market, 2018 to 2026. The core of it is
every bid and offer submitted to the day-ahead auction at unit level — both
sides, accepted and rejected, so the full supply and demand curves rather than
just the outcome. Alongside that, the intraday sessions, prices, generation and
demand for both countries.

The one thing blocking any analysis was that the market operator publishes every
offer but identifies each unit only by a code: no technology, no owner. Without
those, neither *which technology sets the price* nor *how much of the market a
firm controls* can be asked at all. Reconstructing them from plant-name text got
me to roughly 41% of volume, which was not enough.

I wrote to OMIE explaining the problem, and they provided their own registry
documentation — twelve dated listings giving each unit's technology and owning
company. That has taken coverage to around **97%** for both, and it also
confirmed several ownership attributions I had inferred indirectly and wasn't
confident in.

On the question itself I've deliberately held off, so that it could be chosen
against what the data can actually identify rather than the other way round.
Two candidates now look feasible: whether the growth of renewables changes the
market power of individual firms — answerable descriptively, but with no natural
treatment and control group; and whether the 2021 clawback on non-emitting
generation changed hydro's opportunity cost of storing water rather than
generating — which does have a control group, since Portuguese plant sits in the
same market but outside the Spanish measure, though it would need a structural
model. I'd rather settle this with you than commit on my own.

I have a short deck with what this shows — happy to send it ahead or walk
through it whenever suits you.
