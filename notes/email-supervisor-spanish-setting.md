**Subject:** Progress on the Spanish setting

Dear [Professor],

Following your advice, I've spent the last stretch on the Spanish setting rather
than on the question. It was the right order — several things I had assumed were
wrong, and two data problems that were blocking everything are now solved.

**On the institutions.** Spain and Portugal do not run two markets that trade
with each other; they run one day-ahead auction clearing a single merged supply
curve, and take different prices only when the interconnection binds (5% of
hours in 2018, 15% by 2026). Portugal therefore shares Spain's prices but not
its regulator — useful, as it turns out.

I also had to correct the dates of the two emergency interventions. The 2021
clawback on non-emitting generation did not end in March 2022 as usually
reported; it was extended four times and ran to December 2023, so it **overlaps
the Iberian gas cap by 18.5 months**. The same decree that introduced it also
suspended the 7% generation tax, so it is a bundle rather than one treatment,
and Portugal zeroed its own clawback over almost exactly the same window. None
of this was in the secondary sources — it came from the consolidated statutes.

**On the data.** OMIE publishes unit-level offers but not which technology or
which company each unit belongs to, which blocked any question about who sets
the price or about firm-level market power. After writing to them, I now have
their own registry: technology coverage went from 41% to **97%** of volume, and
firm ownership likewise. It also confirmed several ownership attributions I had
inferred indirectly and wasn't confident in.

With that in place, hydro turns out to set the clearing price more often than
any other technology in every year except the 2022 gas crisis.

send it ahead or walk through it whenever suits you.
